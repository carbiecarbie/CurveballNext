## 2. Product contract and recommended defaults

**Confirmed:** private online 1×1 through an invite link or room code. Exactly **THREE LIVES per player**. A missed return removes **one life from that player**. Zero lives ends the match with that player losing. No sets, best-of, ranked score, level progression or campaign. Existing Classic remains the accepted solo game, including its different starting lives and progression.

The following are explicit M5 recommendations, available for review rather than hidden inheritances:

| Decision | Proposed behavior and reason |
|---|---|
| Match parameters | Named immutable `online-v1` configuration: 30 Hz, depth speed magnitude 2 units/tick and curve divisor 25 (the accepted level-1 physical coefficients), both humans use easing 1.5. No `level`, AI divisor, score buckets or difficulty progression. Slow initial flight gives this first private release a conservative network budget; changing coefficients later is a separate multiplayer decision. |
| Initial serve | Server chooses A or B with one unbiased random bit when the first match starts; publish the choice. Room creator gets no guaranteed first-serve advantage. Store the bit in the ephemeral diagnostic record, never draw randomness per tick. |
| Countdown and launch | Both ready starts a 90-tick / 3-second countdown. Center both paddles once on countdown entry, clear displacement/history, place the stationary ball at the serving end's center. Paddles are locked during countdown; discard mouse samples and pending targets rather than retaining a target for launch. Server launches automatically away from the serving side at countdown expiry. Movement requires a newly captured eligible Rally sample under the phase policy below. No click-to-serve or indefinite serve wait online. |
| Launch curve | In the server's serving-player view use the accepted centered stationary serve minimum `cx=-0.01, cy=-0.01`, zero lateral velocity, and outward depth speed 2. Transform to canonical coordinates. Inputs cannot add a serve bonus; this replaces solo's interactive cached serve specifically for online. |
| Nonterminal miss | Commit one life loss on the detecting tick; stop the ball and show the miss for 19 ticks (accepted hold duration, about 633 ms). Then increment rally ID, center/reset both paddles and ball, clear pending movement and curve, and start another 90-tick countdown. The player who missed serves next, toward the opponent. |
| Terminal miss | Commit loss and `MatchEnded` atomically on the same tick at zero lives. Skip restart/countdown. A client shows the winner once it has validated and applied a final result; server finalization alone does not establish client knowledge. No later input or delayed event can reopen that match. See §6. |
| Evolution within rally | Keep accepted lateral acceleration, decay, wall attenuation, lateral-velocity retention and depth reflection. Speed magnitude along z stays 2; lateral speed can evolve through curve. On every human return, install curve from the prior authoritative paddle displacement using the symmetric rule in §4. No hit-count acceleration or time-based difficulty. |
| Simultaneous events | One ball can cross only one end per tick with the fixed depth speed. Walls resolve y then x before the single end contact. Life loss is unique per `(matchId,rallyId)`. If an invariant ever yields two terminal losers, abort as a server fault; do not import solo's enemy-depletion priority or invent a draw rule. |
| Rematch | Both occupied, connected players must independently accept within 60 seconds. New match ID, three lives each, reset ball/paddle/sequence/pending-input state while preserving each socket's control generation and fenced/enabled status; alternate the previous match's **initial** serving side. A rematch cannot reopen blurred control. No series tally. Rematch agreement itself supplies readiness, followed by the same countdown. |
| Pause / interruptions | Online has no unilateral gameplay pause, retry, debug miss or step. Ordinary visible blur keeps the match running with the last accepted target and normal easing; genuine misses cost lives. Control fencing and fresh-state return are specified in §6. Leave, hidden page, connection loss or detected stalled runtime releases a pre-match slot or aborts an unfinished match without winner. Server-finalized and client-known results have distinct immutable-result rules in §6. |

Resetting both paddles and auto-serving are deliberate online lifecycle choices; they do not reproduce M4's retained-paddle/Load callback timeline. Symmetric human contact handling is also explicitly online behavior. The accepted Classic entry, controls, coefficients, callback order, lives, scoring and presentation remain intact.

**Narrow maintainer-authorized amendment, 1 October 2026 — non-rally controls (pending independent implementation review).** This replaces the previous countdown target-retention rule. Capture, sending, authoritative target consumption and mouse-driven local prediction operate only in **Rally**, with the existing §6 generation/enable handshake satisfied. Waiting stays at its authoritative initial pose; Countdown centers once on entry and stays stationary; LifeLostHold retains the authoritative miss pose until the prescribed countdown reset; MatchEnded, Aborted and closing remain stationary. Outside Rally discard samples, unsent/pending targets and prediction history. A Rally transition cannot reuse the current cursor or reopen fenced control: require a newly captured sample after runtime processing of an ordinary current-Rally snapshot and control enablement. Each input's processedSnapshotSerial must identify an issued ordinary current-Rally snapshot, so a countdown sample delivered after launch is discarded. At launch neutralize any obsolete target to the existing paddle position without another position reset. Visible blur continues easing toward the last applied target **during Rally**; non-rally phases remain stationary, with all deadlines unchanged. Preserve the actual preceding incoming draw pose, frozen through the buffered contact sample under §5; then install the authoritative static pose, without moving prediction or substituting corrected geometry in the evidence. Focus/closure safety retains priority. Never move the browser/OS pointer. No physics, numerical, life, serve, hold, liveness, fairness or release gate is amended.

Minimal title choices: **Classic** and **Online · Private 1×1**. Online exposes Create, Join code, Copy invitation, Ready, Leave, result and Rematch. ## 3. Architecture and transport decision

**Recommend one authoritative TypeScript/Node process on one Fly Machine in `gru` (São Paulo), using native browser WebSocket over TLS and a small server WebSocket library. Keep the static frontend on its existing Cloudflare host.** In-memory room map only; no database, accounts, persistent recovery or separate room service.

| Option | Ownership, benefits and costs | M5 decision |
|---|---|---|
| Node authority + WSS | Server owns targets accepted per tick, paddle motion, ball, collisions, lives, lifecycle and time. Clients cannot submit a hit, position, winner or clock advance. One runtime resolves disagreement. Regional VM gives explicit placement and ordinary timers; operator owns process health and resource caps. | Recommended. Simple isolated deployment, direct TypeScript reuse and predictable Brazil location. Single-process failure aborts rooms. |
| Worker router + one Durable Object per room | DO supplies one authority/serialization point and WebSockets; room isolation and scaling are attractive. Ordinary Workers alone do not provide a reliable shared in-memory authority across requests. Continuous room simulation incurs duration and prevents idle hibernation. | Credible alternative, but Durable Objects' documented South America placement limitation and `sam` fallback to Eastern North America weaken expected Brazil latency for this option. This is not a claim that all Cloudflare authority runs in North America. [Cloudflare location documentation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/reference/data-location/) documents the restriction. |
| Browser host + WebRTC DataChannel | Host owns the full match, guest sends targets. Potential direct path and reduced simulation hosting cost; host can cheat, sees lower local latency, and suspension/exit kills the match. A signaling service exchanges SDP/ICE; STUN discovers routes and TURN relay fallback is needed for restrictive NAT/firewalls. Relay service/bandwidth and short-lived TURN credentials still cost effort/money. | Reject for M5: more failure paths and unequal authority for little benefit over a two-player server. No host migration. Signaling is outside WebRTC itself; see [official WebRTC connection guide](https://webrtc.org/getting-started/peer-connections). |
| Peer lockstep / rollback | Both peers simulate; lockstep delays progress for input, rollback needs state history and contact/lifecycle correction. Same-engine solo replay does not establish cross-browser bit-identical results or fair timestamp assignment. Host cheating remains unless a separate authority verifies. | Reject. More complexity than the state size warrants. |

WSS supplies reliable, ordered delivery over TCP; packet loss can hold later messages behind retransmission, rather than delivering fresh game state first. Handle staleness explicitly. HTTP polling adds avoidable per-message overhead/latency; SSE needs a second uplink and gains nothing here. WebRTC permits unordered/limited-retransmission DataChannels, but adds ICE/signaling/TURN and server-side stack complexity for an authoritative design. WebTransport offers streams/datagrams, but adds another server/deployment path without evidence that this tiny first release needs it. Sources: [WebSocket RFC](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc6455/), [WebRTC specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/webrtc/), [WebTransport specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/webtransport/).

Use native browser `WebSocket`, Node HTTP upgrade and `ws` server-side; pin a reviewed version during authorized implementation. The [upstream ws documentation](https://github.com/websockets/ws) covers upgrade authentication, broken-connection detection and notes compression's resource overhead. Disable per-message compression initially. Socket.IO/Colyseus are unnecessary for two slots and a bounded schema; their convenience does not remove authority, timing or lifecycle work. No dependency is installed by this plan.

Authority prevents fabricated ball/life outcomes and invalid paddle movement; it does not prevent aim bots, distributed room spam or a malicious player abandoning a losing match. That is acceptable for private unranked play. Origin validation is a browser safeguard, not authentication against custom clients.

## 4. Canonical world, perspective and contact symmetry

Use one canonical world: A defends `z=0`, B defends `z=75`; x increases right in A's view and y increases down. Field center is `(175.5,125.5)`. Geometric transformations below describe real-coordinate relationships; the arithmetic/storage contract following the table controls their implementation.

| Quantity | A view | B view (180° around vertical axis) |
|---|---|---|
| Position | `(x,y,z)` | `(351-x, y, 75-z)` |
| Ball velocity | `(vx,vy,vz)` | `(-vx,vy,-vz)` |
| Curve | `(cx,cy)` | `(-cx,cy)` |
| Paddle displacement | `(dx,dy)` | `(-dx,dy)` |
| Input local → canonical | `(localX,localY)` | `(351-localX,localY)` |

The reflection uses **351**, twice the field center, not the 350-pixel stage width. Y is not inverted; mouse right/down moves one's near paddle right/down. Each viewer sees their own cyan near paddle at depth 0, opponent green at depth 75. Transform directional feedback consistently; audio/life labels use local roles. Never mirror an already projected or quantized rectangle.

### Numerical representation and operation order

All authoritative logical arithmetic is ECMAScript binary64, with explicit operations and no algebraic reassociation. Target, position, previous position and displacement of **each paddle remain in its owning player's local view**, including in snapshots and prediction rebases. Canonical paddle coordinates are derived observations, never stored movement/history inputs. Consequently `351-(351-localX)` is forbidden on the own-paddle path. No quantized position feeds logical movement.

For each player independently, in this order:

1. Apply `stagePoint` to a newly captured mouse sample, validate the local target bounds, then the accepted `display.readInput` in local space: multiply by 20, truncate/clamp to signed twips, float32 input readback and ties-to-even integer-pixel rounding in its existing operation order. Store this local target without reflection.
2. Using the same old local x/y, compute y first, then x: `newY = max(45,min(206,oldY-(oldY-targetY)/1.5))`; `newX = max(55,min(296,oldX-(oldX-targetX)/1.5))`. Store no intermediate quantization. Displacement is `newX-previousLocalX`, `newY-previousLocalY`; then set previousLocal to new local position. Prior published samples remain available to the current ball callback as specified below.
3. For the own near box use the existing `project(newX,newY,0,60,40)` expression **including** `center + (position-center)*scale(0)`, then `display.install`. Do not assume this projection is numerically an identity or replace it with direct installation. Installation truncates center×20 to integer twips; half extents use the existing nested `Math.fround` operations and ties-to-even rounding. Retain integer-twip bounds before the installer's pixel division as the comparison representation, with pixel divisions only for drawing/export; never recover integer edges by multiplying/truncating exported pixel bounds.
4. Compute curve from prior **local** displacement first, then convert signs. A's `cx=-localDx/25`, B's canonical `cx=-(-localDx/25)`; both `cy=localDy/25`. Never derive displacement by subtracting two reflected positions. Rendering the remote paddle uses its stored owning-local offset `(localX-175.5,localY-125.5)`, sign-reverses the horizontal offset once, multiplies by the depth scale, adds the center, then installs. Own near rendering uses step 3. Neither path round-trips through absolute canonical x.

The ball needs a narrow **online-only numerical adaptation**: store canonical horizontal offset `u=x-175.5`, not absolute x as recurrent state. At creation/reset u=0. Keep absolute y, integer z (with the fixed depth speed 2), vx/vy/vz/cx/cy. Tick operation order is `vx+=cx; vy+=cy; z+=vz; u+=vx; y-=vy`, then curve decay, y-wall response, x-wall response and contact. Y walls retain accepted semantics. Horizontal wall comparisons use strict `u<-135.5` or `u>135.5`, then clamp to that bound, attenuate cx and reverse vx in accepted order. Derived x=`175.5+u` is for observation only and is never subtracted back into u. This changes tiny online arithmetic rounding paths explicitly; it changes no coefficient, box extent, hit tolerance or Classic implementation.

Resolve both ball projections from that same canonical sample. For A use sign +1 and depth z; B sign −1 and depth `75-z`. Evaluate `g=scale(localDepth)` by the accepted atan expression, then `screenX=175.5+(sign*u)*g`, `screenY=125.5+(y-125.5)*g`, sizes `30*g`; run the accepted installer separately on those poses. Sign reversal precedes multiplication and center addition; never first materialize absolute x, then subtract/reflect it. Both contact and snapshot-endpoint rendering use this pipeline. Depth stays integral within the bounded crossing range under online-v1, so the depth reflection is exact. Exchanging players and negating u/vx/cx executes the same local projection operands at the opposite end. Snapshots carry canonical u and owning-local paddle values; client view inversion compares the original canonical sample, not a lossy reconstructed x round trip. Signed zero is normalized in serialized observations, without affecting comparisons.

**Contact rule:** preserve old-box and prior-paddle timing, evaluated in the defender's near view. At each tick end retain both ball projections and each paddle's own near box. On strict crossing (`z<0` or `z>75`), compare that defender's previous ball and paddle **integer-twip** intervals with closed AABB inequalities, without epsilon. Do not replace the old box with integrated geometry or accept a rendered overlap. Reuse the accepted installer and overlap semantics; the online ball-offset projection is the explicit adaptation above. Logical analytic comparisons may use M4's existing abs 1e-9 + rel 1e-12 tolerance; discrete bounds and hit/miss must be exactly equal in mirrored fixtures. A logical tolerance cannot excuse a different installed edge.

This makes A and B use equivalent near-player contact geometry; the solo enemy's far-view contact proxy is deliberately not the human-B rule. A single A-view installed box for both players would encode observer-dependent asymmetry. Keep two contact proxies as server simulation data, not as client-render authority. Mirror fixtures must include fractional/twip edge cases and diagonal corners.

**Curve rule:** use the local-displacement operation order above for either defender; calling `installCurve(..., enemy=true)` for human B is forbidden. Returns retain canonical vx/vy and reverse vz as today. Stationary returns install zero curve. Serve floors apply only to the explicit centered automatic launch in §2.

Server tick order: resolve due lifecycle → validate/coalesce available targets → snapshot prior paddle samples → ball integration/decay/walls/contact/life transaction → move both paddles using the same easing/clamp → install next contact boxes → emit events/snapshot. The two human moves must be independent, so slot order cannot favor a side. Newly received input does not retroactively move the paddle used by the current tick's contact. This adds one accepted prior-publication tick to the network control path and must be included in latency tests.

## 5. Timing, responsiveness and protocol

### Fixed simulation and rendering

Run the server at **30 fixed steps/second**, deriving due ticks from a monotonic epoch. Timers wake the scheduler; they are not themselves the clock. No variable-dt conversion, client-driven advance, or 60 Hz physics retuning. Allow at most five due ticks per wake; if six or more are overdue, abort affected active matches as `server-overrun`, rebase idle scheduling, and stop accepting new matches until healthy. Do not silently fast-forward or award losses after a long process stall.

Send a **full compact snapshot after every tick (30 Hz)** during Countdown/Rally/LifeLostHold, plus authoritative lifecycle results; Waiting/MatchEnded need only state changes and fresh 1 Hz application heartbeat messages. Room tick is monotonic across rallies/rematches and advances only in ticking phases; server-message serial advances in every phase. Each snapshot supplies canonical ball u, both owning-local paddle samples and server-computed integer-twip endpoint boxes for both views. Rendering interpolates these endpoints; it does not recompute the ball from a reflected absolute x. Snapshot design target remains 1 KiB typical/2 KiB hard maximum; qualify actual payloads against the retained cost assumptions.

Render at `requestAnimationFrame` at any display rate. Estimate server-clock offset from runtime-processed probes as specified in §9, favoring the lowest-RTT samples in the last 20; smooth offset adjustments without moving rendered time backward. Ball and opponent interpolate at `estimatedServerNow - (smoothedRTT/2 + 66.7 ms)`, with total delay bounded to 66.7–166.7 ms. The buffer adds **two ticks / 66.7 ms beyond estimated transit time**; at symmetric 100 ms RTT nominal ball age is 116.7 ms. Actual snapshot age, interpolation underflow, display scheduling and asymmetric-path uncertainty are separate observations. Never interpolate across rally/match IDs or through an impact. If a next endpoint is unavailable, hold; at snapshot age >250 ms show degradation, >500 ms visibly freeze world rendering. Authority continues until its failure policy applies.

Lifecycle state/lives/result knowledge update immediately when validated and applied by the runtime. World impact/miss/terminal discontinuities and their audio follow the buffered event tick. Before installing a contact discontinuity at tick C, present the final incoming old ball proxy from C−1 with the actually drawn local paddle for at least one frame, then snap on the following frame. Do not blend through the wall/paddle or obscure this frame with the terminal overlay; a result label outside the court may update immediately, and the overlay follows that frame. Contact events include the incoming old-box tick/bounds so the buffer can retain that pose even if the event arrives early. The delay is bounded to the existing world buffer plus one display frame, adds no speculative ball trajectory, and never postpones authoritative lives or finalization. This explicit presentation ordering prevents immediate HUD/discontinuity handling from erasing the fairness sample in §9. Reset clears obsolete buffers only after the preceding incoming-contact frame is consumed; stale/failed transport remains visibly interrupted rather than fabricating a frame.

Predict **only the local paddle during enabled Rally control**, using the §4 owning-local operation order at 30 Hz, then interpolate its visual pose each display frame. Retain at most 60 inputs/two seconds. Rebase from the server's owning-local sample and consumed-input acknowledgement; replay only unacknowledged inputs in the same current control generation and Rally context. Non-rally phases discard this history under the §2 amendment. Blur clears this history; return uses the §6 fresh-state handshake and a new mouse sample. Arrival-to-tick assignment can differ, so prediction is approximate visual feedback, not exact rollback. Blend corrections ≤4 native pixels over ≤100 ms; larger corrections snap. Retain the incoming draw history and its pre-discontinuity local visual/prediction pose before lifecycle-specific clearing/rebase. Hold that incoming local pose through the sampled frame, then apply event-triggered prediction snap/clear and phase/rally draw resets on the following frame. Immediate authoritative knowledge/lives still update; ordinary snapshot corrections before the boundary run normally and their pre/post geometry is logged. Focus/unavailable control safety takes its own priority and cannot be delayed for sampling. Neither prediction nor blend feeds authoritative contact boxes.

Fast local feedback does not establish collision agreement. At a symmetric 100 ms RTT, the modeled ball age 50+66.7=116.7 ms, upstream input transit 50 ms and one prior-box publication tick 33.3 ms already total about 200 ms. Adding independent 0–33.3ms send wait and 0–33.3ms server tick-assignment wait produces the reviewed **approximately 200–266.7 ms** interval from the shown ball time to the first possible input-driven contact-box effect. This is a modeled scenario, not measured capability, a universal bound or time to reach the target. Snapshot gaps/loss, rendering delay, scheduler jitter, clock uncertainty and multi-tick easing may add mismatch; measure them individually. A correct server can reject an apparent contact even when the local paddle responds in ≤50 ms. The operational classification and quantitative gate in §9 address that distinction.

On an authoritative rejection, install the real miss/lives immediately, preserve the incoming frame and local visual pose as above, then clear incompatible prediction/feedback after that frame; never change authority to agree with the drawing. Audio is played once only for authoritative events at their presentation boundary. Full snapshots restore state if an event is missing.

**No rewind lag compensation in M5.** Rewinding paddles for late inputs changes a shared ball path and can invalidate the other player's already observed return. Client timestamps cannot safely grant arbitrary historical contact. Low regional RTT, local paddle prediction and bounded visual delay are the first approach. If physical acceptance fails at the target envelope, reopen this targeted decision with measurements; do not silently add a rollback system or tune Classic.

**Maintainer-authorized amendment, 1 October 2026 — bounded defender contact claim (pending independent review).** Measured evidence: all eight controlled cells (C0–C5b, 300/defender) recorded 49–77 clear apparent-contact rejections, concentrated at the 50 ms capture margin even on C0. With immediate local prediction (≤50 ms response gate) and a ball drawn ≥66.7 ms in the past, the incoming frame pairs the ball at C−1 with a paddle that already includes inputs the authority could not yet apply, so the ≤1% gate, the response gate and "no rewind" cannot all hold. The maintainer chose a bounded grace window, replacing "no rewind" only for the defender's own end-plane contact decision:

- When a tick would end a Rally by a miss/finish for side d whose session is enabled, the authority does not commit it. It pauses that room before the transaction (no tick, no input consumption, unchanged deadlines elsewhere), keeps sending unchanged snapshots and sends side d a `contactPending` frame with the tick and incoming C−1 view boxes. An authoritative contact is still committed immediately; walls, scoring and physics are unchanged.
- When the defender's render timeline reaches that transaction, its client draws its own paddle at exactly the predicted pose (no blend), evaluates the same closed twip inequality against the incoming C−1 ball box and sends one `contactClaim` {tick, hit, x, y, dx, dy}, first flushing any unsent target. That frame is the retained incoming evidence frame.
- The authority resolves at the next boundary after the claim or after `contactGraceMs` = 500 ms. A hit claim is accepted only for the same match/rally/tick/side/control generation and only if x/y lie within the axis envelope spanned by the authoritative C−1 paddle and the targets that session sent within the last 1000 ms, and |dx|, |dy| do not exceed one easing step across that envelope. The authoritative paddle is then set to the claimed pose before the transaction runs, so the ordinary deterministic step decides contact and curve. A miss claim, an invalid claim or the timeout commits the original miss.
- The opponent sees the ball held at the defender's plane for the pause (normally RTT/2 + render delay ≈ 100–270 ms; at most 500 ms). Because the opponent's own view is already ≥66.7 ms delayed, an accepted claim never revises a return or miss that either player has already been shown. Client timestamps are not trusted; only claimed poses that the server's received targets make reachable are. Cheating risk is bounded by that envelope and accepted for private rooms.
- All §9 gates are unchanged; the controlled cells must be rerun. Physics, thresholds, network envelope and Classic are unchanged.

### Wire outline and trust boundary

Use versioned JSON (`protocolVersion=1`, `rulesVersion=online-v1`). Every message includes protocolVersion; bound messages include roomEpoch, omitted from the abbreviated table below. Every server application message, including joined, events, errors and control acknowledgements, also carries the increasing per-socket serverSerial and monotonic serverTime in a common envelope. Joined binds the socket's room epoch; subsequent frames must match it. Serial/time metadata alone does not make an error/control acknowledgement a valid health proof. The epoch is a random per-room generation, independent of the public code. All schemas reject unknown message types, unexpected fields, non-finite numbers, unsafe integers and oversize frames. Protocol mismatch is an actionable reload/update error, never best-effort fallback.

| Direction | Message and fields |
|---|---|
| Client → server, unbound socket | `create {requestId,probeId,c0}` or `join {requestId,roomCode,probeId,c0}`. One operation per socket, within 5 seconds of upgrade; probe values bootstrap diagnostic clock alignment, never authority. |
| Server → client | `joined {roomId,slot,playerSessionCredential,roomEpoch,protocolVersion,rulesVersion,probeId,echoC0,s1,s2,controlGeneration,enabled,initialStateSerial,freshSnapshot}` sent only to that socket. The enclosed initial state serial authorizes generation 0 control; s1/s2 are server receive/send times for this join probe. |
| Client → server, bound socket | `ready {requestId,value}`, `input {matchId,rallyId,controlGeneration,resumeStateSerial,seq,x,y,processedSnapshotSerial}`, `rematch {requestId,matchId,value}`, `leave {requestId}`, `unavailable {reason}`, `controlFence {generation,requestId}`, `controlSync {generation,requestId}`, `controlResume {generation,stateSerial,matchId,rallyId,requestId}`. |
| Client → server, runtime heartbeat | `heartbeat {runtimeBeatSeq,processedServerSerial,processedSnapshotSerial,processedTick,matchId,rallyId,phase,echoNonce,probeId,c0,c3}`; acknowledgements mean state applied by the game runtime. c0 starts a new diagnostic probe; c3 records runtime processing of the previously echoed probe. Probe timestamps are diagnostic, not input authority. |
| Server → clients | `snapshot {serverSerial,serverTime,matchId,rallyId,tick,phase,phaseDeadline,servingSide,lives[2],localPaddles[2],ball:{u,y,z,vx,vy,vz,cx,cy},viewBoxes,controlGeneration[2],ackInputSeq[2],ackInputTick[2],lastEventId,result}`. `viewBoxes` contains integer-twip render/contact endpoints; local samples include previous/displacement. |
| Server → clients | `event {serverSerial,matchId,rallyId,eventId,tick,type,side,incomingBoxTick,incomingViewBoxes,lives,result}`, `error {serverSerial,requestId,code}`, `heartbeat {serverSerial,nonce,serverTime,echoProbeId,s1,s2,echoC0}`, `controlAck {serverSerial,generation,requestId,kind,enabled,freshSnapshot}`. Heartbeat carries a new health nonce; when replying to the latest client probe it also echoes its identity/c0 with server receive s1 and send s2 (omit that tuple if no new probe exists). Final result includes immutable match/event identity, winner, losing side and final lives. |

Credentials are random 256-bit per-slot capabilities, distinct from public room IDs. The initial authenticated context is the newly assigned socket; credential is held only in client memory/server memory, never URL, logs, opponent snapshot, durable storage or bundle. Subsequent commands are accepted only on that bound socket; a supplied slot/credential on a different socket cannot steal it. No resume endpoint exists in M5. The capability provides explicit session identity without becoming an account or promising reconnection. Discard it on close/expiry.

Send latest pointer target at at most 30 Hz, even if the browser produces hundreds of events. Local mouse motion remains immediate for prediction. Online targets are constrained to a documented finite local stage-plus-margin rectangle `x∈[-350,700], y∈[-250,500]`; ease then clamp paddle center to accepted field limits. This permits outside-court aiming while bounding pathological targets. Both sides use identical limits. Do not preclamp Classic input or accept velocities/displacements from clients.

For each player retain only the newest pending `seq` in the enabled current control generation. Assign it to the next unprocessed tick at receipt; coalesce arrivals before that boundary. No client timestamp changes acceptance. Ignore duplicate/older sequences, control generations and match/rally epochs; reject future generations except the next valid fence. Sequence resets on new match ID, while control generation and its fenced/enabled status persist through rematches on that socket. Generation 0 inputs use joined's initialStateSerial as resumeStateSerial; a resumed generation uses the state serial authorized by its completed resume handshake. This generation authorization remains valid until its next fence, including through rematch, while each input independently requires a processedSnapshotSerial actually sent to that socket/current match/rally. Future claims or snapshots >15 room ticks old trigger resync/discard. A claim is not anti-cheat proof. Processed state also drives liveness under §6; network receipt alone cannot acknowledge it.

Lifecycle commands use request IDs with a bounded 64-entry response cache per session. Set-ready/rematch operations are idempotent. Events increment server IDs; clients apply each once. Full snapshots carry current lives/result, so reconnect-like transport assumptions are not needed for correctness. A life decrement and any finish event are committed in one synchronous room transition, with no awaited I/O inside it.

### Backpressure and observables

Do not queue every obsolete snapshot/input. Keep one unsent latest snapshot and one pending target per player. When socket `bufferedAmount` exceeds 64 KiB, skip replaceable state writes, retain a bounded lifecycle-event queue (maximum 32), and flag degradation. At 256 KiB, queue overflow, or sustained congestion for 2 seconds, close/abort the session. Client drops unsent intermediate targets too. Bytes already handed to TCP cannot be withdrawn; stale receipt limits still apply. The [browser WebSocket standard](https://websockets.spec.whatwg.org/) exposes `bufferedAmount` for this purpose.

Use compact in-memory counters and optional user-triggered redacted diagnostic export, not a hosted analytics service: RTT and jitter, snapshot age, server tick lateness/CPU duration, input receipt/applied/contact tick, skipped snapshots, queue size, local prediction correction in native pixels, last event IDs and terminal reason. Compare authoritative state at the **same tick**, not simultaneous screenshot wall times. Retain at most 600 ticks of sampled diagnostic records per active room; clear on cleanup and never include credentials/IPs in exports.

## 6. Bounded room and match lifecycle

One process owns all rooms. Slot allocation and every transition are synchronous; no await between capacity check and claim. A valid invitation never guarantees an available slot.

| State | Entry / allowed action | Exit |
|---|---|---|
| `Waiting` | Creator initially takes A; first guest takes B. After a pre-match departure, a new join takes the vacant slot without moving the remaining player. Both default not-ready. Sharing link does not share a session. | Both present and ready → `Countdown`; leave/disconnect removes that slot, resets remaining readiness. Empty → delete. |
| `Countdown` | Publish serving side and tick deadline. Initialize lives 3/3 only for a new match; after a lost life preserve the decremented totals. Locked paddles and stationary ball. Ready toggles no longer cancel countdown. | At deadline → `Rally`; interruption → `Aborted`. |
| `Rally` | Only server steps and bounded targets affect gameplay. | Miss with remaining lives → `LifeLostHold`; zero → `MatchEnded`; interruption → `Aborted`. |
| `LifeLostHold` | One already-committed loss, ball stopped, no further contact/loss. | At +19 ticks reset and enter `Countdown` for next rally; interruption → `Aborted`. |
| `MatchEnded` | Immutable zero-life result; accept/withdraw rematch request. | Both agree → new match `Countdown`; leave, disconnect or 60-second deadline → close room. |
| `Aborted` | No contest, show reason, preserve last committed lives as diagnostic only. No winner inferred. | Notify connected peer, close sockets, delete room; user may explicitly create a new room. |

### Ordinary visible blur and control-generation fence

Settled revised proposal for maintainer approval: ordinary blur while the page remains visible and its runtime healthy **does not pause, abort or extend the match**. This allows Alt+Tab/clicking elsewhere without imposing a match penalty beyond the normal risk of an unattended paddle. The server keeps the last target already accepted into paddle state and applies ordinary easing; actual misses still remove lives. Pending, not-yet-applied targets are discarded by the fence. Blur does not grant extra time, reset counters or exempt a throttled/frozen runtime from failure detection.

Control generation is a safe monotonic integer per bound socket, initially 0 and retained across rallies/rematches. Initial control is enabled. Each ordinary blur performs the following protocol; use this single handshake, not alternate resume paths:

1. Client synchronously disables capture/sending/prediction, increments desired generation g, discards unsent targets and all pending prediction history, then sends `controlFence(g,requestId)`. Movement arriving while unfocused is discarded. Return does not reuse `latestPointer` or accumulated events.
2. Server processes inbound messages in socket arrival order in the room's serialized execution. A next-generation fence (`g=current+1`) immediately sets generation g, clears all pending old inputs and disables control. Duplicate g fences return the cached acknowledgement; older fences are ignored, larger jumps rejected. Keep the last already-applied target. Inputs accepted/applied before this fence are not retroactively undone; an old target queued before the fence but not yet applied is removed. After the fence, reject all old-generation input/resume messages and all input while disabled. The fence acknowledgement includes g, disabled status and fresh state.
3. On visible focus return, keep control disabled and send ordered `controlSync(g,requestId)`. The server handles it only after the fence and returns `controlAck(kind=sync,g,freshSnapshot)` for current match/rally. The runtime validates/applies this snapshot and rebases its own local paddle with empty prediction history, then sends `controlResume(g,stateSerial,matchId,rallyId,requestId)` acknowledging that exact processed state. Server accepts only a sync-issued state for current g/context no more than 15 room ticks old; otherwise reply resync/current state without enabling control.
4. Server enables g and acknowledges `controlAck(kind=resume,enabled=true)`. Client resumes only after its runtime processes the matching acknowledgement while still visibly focused. Accept the **first newly captured mouse sample after this acknowledgement/return**, then send it with g and acknowledged resume-state serial. No sample retained before/during blur or the handshake can become the resumed target. Until that new sample arrives, server retains its prior target. The conservative handshake adds about two round trips after return; it is a deliberate input-safety tradeoff, not a match-clock pause.

Rapid blur→return→blur increments again and queues the next fence on the same ordered socket. Old sync/resume acknowledgements cannot reopen local control: check current desired g, request ID, focus/visibility and context. A return message processed before a later fence may temporarily enable that old server generation, but no local input is emitted while the client is unfocused; the later fence clears anything still pending. Messages processed before a fence remain subject to normal rules. If rally/match changes during the handshake, obtain a new current sync; while terminal/aborted/closed, no gameplay resume. A new current nonterminal Waiting/Countdown/match state received while visibly focused but still fenced triggers the same-generation fresh-state handshake, even without another focus event. Thus a focus return during MatchEnded followed by rematch cannot leave control permanently fenced: reacquire through sync/resume acknowledgements and a new post-ack mouse sample, never automatic enabling or retained movement. This trigger resets no health or match timer. Rate-limit focus traffic under the existing control bucket; excessive toggling cannot bypass bounds or refresh liveness. Generations and request caches remain bounded; close before safe-integer exhaustion.

While visibly blurred, process authoritative state and runtime heartbeats normally and draw the own paddle from authoritative samples rather than accumulated prediction. Thirty seconds of responsive visible blur is permitted; five seconds of missing runtime progress is not. Focus fences/acks never reset five-second liveness, the two-second sustained-congestion timer, match/countdown deadlines or the absolute one-hour room lifetime.

**Minimal reconnect policy: none.** Socket close/error or hidden page releases its Waiting slot and clears remaining readiness; during Countdown/Rally/LifeLostHold it aborts without winner when detected. Refresh/new tab cannot reclaim an active player. No host migration, refresh token, checkpoint or resume grace. A premature pre-match refresh join may receive Full until detection releases the old slot. New socket means new identity. After MatchEnded, close the room/rematch path while respecting client-known results below.

### Phase-aware runtime liveness, one-way failure and cleanup

The network handler only enqueues parsed frames. An acknowledgement is emitted after the game runtime validates epoch/version/context/order, applies the state to its authoritative view and updates its processed serial/tick. Receiving bytes, updating a socket callback counter or automatic WebSocket pongs does not establish healthy runtime progress. Server application messages have increasing per-socket serials; its fresh heartbeat nonce/time is issued every ≤1 second in all phases. Client runtime emits an increasing runtimeBeatSeq heartbeat every ≤1 second after processing state/that challenge.

Server starts `lastValidRuntimeBeat` at its known session-bind/joined-send time and requires fresh runtime beats in **every phase**; it cannot observe joined delivery. A valid beat comes from the bound session, current epoch/protocol, a strictly newer beat sequence, a newly runtime-processed and previously unacknowledged issued heartbeat nonce (age ≤5 seconds), and an actually issued processed ordinary snapshot/heartbeat serial newer than its preceding health acknowledgement, with coherent phase/match/rally. Mark that nonce consumed for health when the beat is accepted. Unknown/future acknowledgements, duplicates, reused nonces, stale challenges, prior epochs and malformed messages do not reset timers. Control fence/sync/resume/ack messages and their embedded sync state never refresh health timers or qualify as the beat's health-proof serial; maintain ordinary snapshot/heartbeat progress separately. Legitimate phase transitions in a recently issued state are allowed; receipt of a pointer input alone does not refresh runtime health.

In **Countdown/Rally/LifeLostHold**, separately require a strictly advancing processed snapshot room tick within 5 seconds. Start a new tick-progress deadline at every nonticking→ticking transition, including Waiting→Countdown and MatchEnded→Countdown for rematch; keep it continuous across ticking phases and rally restarts. This phase-specific origin never resets the existing runtime-beat deadline. A delayed acknowledgement counts only for an issued state in the current match and no more than 15 ticks behind the authority. In **Waiting/MatchEnded**, ticks need not advance: fresh runtime beat plus processed current server heartbeat/state serial is sufficient. Entering either nonticking phase disables only the tick-progress requirement, not the runtime-beat timer. A heartbeat must be produced by the state-processing loop even while visibly blurred.

At elapsed **≥5seconds** without a valid runtime beat, or without required ticking progress, detect failure. A monotonic server watchdog runs at ≤100 ms intervals independently of room physics; serialize its detected interruption before the next physics step. Also detect close/error/hidden `unavailable` immediately on processing. No pointer movement is not a failure: retain the last target without velocity extrapolation. A freeze that still produces protocol pongs fails the runtime test. A server process too stalled to run the watchdog instead triggers overrun on resumption; a dead process cannot promise immediate cleanup, so clients have their own watchdog.

Each client starts a five-second server-message deadline at socket open (including join handshake), then resets it only when its runtime processes a fresh valid ordinary heartbeat/snapshot/event (or initial joined response) for that socket/epoch. Require increasing serverSerial, coherent context and a server timestamp whose aligned age is less than 5 seconds; where clock uncertainty straddles that age, request a fresh nonce response and never extend the existing deadline on the uncertain frame. During initial synchronization the fresh echo of this connection's probe establishes validity. Control acknowledgements/errors, pongs, duplicate/stale frames, wrong versions/epochs or invalid state do not count. Check the deadline at least every 100 ms on a responsive runtime and before queued-state/control processing; at elapsed ≥5 seconds interrupt locally, stop controls/prediction and close, preserving any known final result. Five seconds is the expiration threshold, with detection at the first watchdog wake (≤100 ms scheduling allowance on a healthy runtime/server). A frozen JavaScript runtime cannot execute its own watchdog, so the server independently terminates it. On runtime resumption, check elapsed time **before** processing queued messages: stale backlog cannot revive an expired session. A valid final frame queued before closure may still be validated/applied for that match as final-result knowledge, but cannot restore liveness or control.

Thus downstream-only stalls cause the client to interrupt and server to fail processed progress; upstream-only stalls cause server runtime-beat expiry and the client subsequently sees closure or its own deadline. The server can process legitimate misses until interruption is detected/serialized. Client wording: “The match continues until the server detects the interruption.” A repeated focus change never starts a new health deadline. Congestion is independently measured from first sustained threshold crossing (2 seconds); room TTL is independently measured from creation (1 hour), including idle/blur/handshake time.

On detection, release a Waiting slot/readiness or atomically abort an unfinished room, notify a responsive peer, cancel affected timers and clear pending inputs/control data/buffers, revoke affected sessions and release their admission counts within **one second after detection**. Empty/aborted/closed rooms are deleted in that interval; a surviving Waiting player retains its live session and original bounded room expiry. Send close promptly, then forcibly terminate affected server sockets by the same one-second deadline if their handshake stalls. On local close the client immediately discards outbound inputs/prediction, but first validates bounded already-received current-match terminal candidates (at most the existing 32-event cap) before discarding other inbound queues. Such a valid candidate can establish result knowledge, never reopen the socket/control or extend health. Forced server termination handles half-open peers. These are detection/cleanup deadlines, not recovery promises.

Hidden page is distinct from ordinary visible blur: synchronously disable input, discard outbound inputs/prediction, send `unavailable(reason=hidden)` and close; preserve/validate bounded received terminal candidates under the preceding rule. In Waiting release slot/readiness; in unfinished play abort when processed/detected; after a known final result retain that UI. If hidden and blur fire together, hidden wins locally and no focus-return handshake is sent on that closing socket. Healthy heartbeats cannot override a processed hidden/leave request.

### Finalization versus client-known final result

At each tick boundary process already-received leave/hidden/close/detected-timeout decisions before physics. If interruption precedes the last-life contact transaction, abort without winner. If that transaction already committed loss→zero and finish, the **server-finalized** result is immutable and subsequent interruption cannot replace it. Ordinary blur never cancels either transaction. Serialize decisions by actual server processing order; do not infer simultaneous wall-time ordering. If both disconnect before finish, abort once.

A **client-known** result exists only after its runtime validates and applies the finish event or full terminal snapshot, including epoch/match/event identity, winner/loser and final lives (loser exactly zero, winner one to three, consistent with the atomic terminal phase and nonincreasing same-match lives). Receiving bytes or inferring a winner from a partial life counter is insufficient. That known result stays immutable in this session's UI through subsequent blur, hidden page, leave, socket closure or backend failure. A valid already-queued final frame for that socket/match may establish knowledge while closing, with no return to control. Conflicting or stale terminal frames cannot overwrite it; explicit validated new-rematch state starts a new match view.

If the server finalized the last life but crashed before delivering a valid result, each client that never validated/applied it shows **interruption / outcome unknown**. One client may know while the other does not. On restart all in-memory rooms disappear; no inferred opponent victory, result recovery or persistence across refresh. Preserve known results only in the current session's bounded UI memory; no database/storage is added.

Identifiers: generate a cryptographically random **12-character unambiguous Base32 room code (60 bits)**, collision-check in memory and retry; display in three groups of four, normalize case/hyphens on entry. `roomId` in the protocol is this normalized public code. Invitation uses only this code in a URL fragment, e.g. `/#room=ABCD-EFGH-JKLM`; never session credentials. No room enumeration/list endpoint. Invalid syntax gets Invalid code; unknown/deleted code gets Room unavailable or expired (no tombstone database); occupied two-slot room gets Full; running room cannot admit replacements. Creation request retry on the same socket returns its existing response rather than another room.

Bounds (application policies, not provider guarantees):

- Waiting expires 10 minutes after creation regardless of heartbeats. Finished rematch window is 60 seconds. Every room has a hard one-hour lifetime; expiry during play aborts without winner. A rally is not decided by a timeout score.
- Initial friends release: at most 10 live rooms, 20 occupied players, 64 total sockets (including short unauthenticated upgrades). At capacity reject new rooms before allocation; joining an already reserved vacant slot is still allowed. Wait/result rooms count toward room cap.
- Incoming frame ≤1 KiB, nesting depth ≤4, string fields individually bounded. Token buckets per session: inputs 30/s sustained, burst 60; other commands 5/s, burst 10. Drop excess replaceable inputs; close sustained abuse after 2 seconds. Reject malformed/auth-invalid frames immediately.
- Per trusted proxy client IP: create 3/minute burst 3, join attempts 20/minute burst 20, upgrades 30/minute and at most 10 unauthenticated sockets. Shared-NAT users may hit these caps; show a retry delay. Use only the platform-established client IP header, never arbitrary forwarded headers from an untrusted direct path. Cap the expiring limiter map at 10,000 entries and reject new anonymous work when saturated.
- Allowlist the actual frontend Origin and development loopback origins; reject missing/foreign Origin for the public browser service. HTTPS/WSS only in production. No secrets in Vite variables: backend URL and protocol version are public configuration. Render codes/errors as text, not HTML. No third-party scripts are needed.

These bounds reduce accidental load and basic abuse. They are not DDoS protection or a financial hard cap; front-door traffic/egress can still cost money.

### Controlled apparent-contact classification and acceptance gate

An opportunity is one predeclared incoming defensive flight ending at a single authoritative end-plane contact tick C for the tested defender, with one scripted target sequence and one captured incoming presentation boundary. Count it once, regardless of frames/events/returns. Capture the **last incoming frame presented before installing C's return/miss discontinuity** under §5: ball uses the old projected proxy from C−1, while the local paddle uses the actual prediction/reconciliation/interpolation geometry presented at that frame's client time. Record that exact frame, its preceding frame, authoritative C−1/C state and result. This intentionally compares the user's displayed local paddle with the displayed incoming ball, not two authoritative poses or an imagined future paddle. Hidden, frozen or missing/occluded contact frames are not successful observations; resolve the capture or fail the gate.

Use a separate offline/test expected-result method that imports **no production collision, projection, display installer or view-mapping helper**. For authority, derive projected boxes with the specified ordered binary64/f32/twip equations independently, check them against §4 literal interval fixtures, and compare captured integer-twip interval edges by the four written closed inequalities. For apparent geometry, use raw rectangles actually emitted by the draw model after prediction/blending (normalize to 350×250 coordinates; exclude decorative glow, shadow and stroke), compare their exact recorded binary64/rational edge values independently, and corroborate screenshots. This visual predicate is an assessment metric and never replaces authoritative geometry.

Classify as **clear apparent contact rejected** when the incoming frame's intersection lengths are at least one native pixel in both axes and the server records a miss at C. A separation gap of at least one pixel on either axis is clear noncontact; otherwise geometry is **borderline**. Uncertain frame association or clock/capture evidence is **ambiguous**. Report both separately with exact edges and adjacent frames. The one-pixel classification margin addresses perceptual ambiguity; it does not enlarge or shrink server collision boxes. Run deliberate exact-edge/one-twip boundary fixtures separately with their exact hit/miss expectations. Every apparent rejection must be independently traced to timing, geometry, numerical behavior or a defect. Exact evidence can resolve capture/time ambiguity into clear contact or noncontact, but genuine subpixel overlap/gap or touching remains borderline even with known edges. It cannot count toward the 300 nonborderline denominator; do not relabel it as a clean noncontact.

Before a run, lock seeds, delay/loss traces, geometry, controller and sample order. Schedule **300 nonborderline catch-intent opportunities per defender per critical profile**: 100 centered/horizontal/vertical moves, 100 diagonal/curved/wall-assisted flights and 100 late target changes. Balance left/right/up/down and both serve orientations; distribute controller capture margins 50/100/200/400 ms before the modeled contact, and displays 60/120/144 Hz. The independent stimulus specification must predict comfortably overlapping incoming visual geometry; no padding with deliberate misses. Actual clearly noncontact outcomes remain in the denominator and are reported as such. Supplemental borderline/calibration fixtures are not pooled into the 300. A test-only seeded controller may choose inputs but cannot inject hit/miss/life outcomes into production protocol; reset/replay fixtures only between opportunities.
